# Grand Auditorium — the double diamond for the orchestra

**Named by the Composer on 2026-11-13, standing: *Grand Auditorium* is the whole method, and `Grand Auditorium on <field>` is its trigger (`protocols/triggers.md`).** The path of this page keeps the earlier working name, *double diamond*, so no link breaks. The industry term for the shape is still the double diamond; the name of the method here is Grand Auditorium.

Proposed by the Composer on 2026-11-10 and run twice the same day, on the presentation gate and on
Strategy. **Two runs are two findings, not a pattern.** Nothing here is installed; the build is the Workshop's.

## The problem it answers

The Composer's observation: *an Auditorium in a field tends to update the existing world and to answer
How first, missing the chance to ask Why and then How.* The plain Auditorium and the Deep Auditorium
(`protocols/triggers.md`) both start from the pages as they are, or from a purpose he states in one
sentence. A purpose written at the call can already assume the thing stays and only needs improving. The
first attempt at this very pilot did exactly that: it produced twenty-one ideas about the existing gate
before anyone had asked what the gate is for.

## The shape

A double diamond has two rounds of diverging then converging. Here:

| | Diverge | Converge | Ends in |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Diamond 1, Why** | every field's chairs ask what problem they solve, for whom, what would be seen if it were solved, and what the field assumes without checking | the strategy quorum cuts to a short list of problem statements | **a brief the Composer rules on.** Nothing is built before it |
| **Diamond 2, How** | the Deep Auditorium on each field, run against the brief and not against the current pages | the quorum merges, labels and ranks, with dependencies and conflicts | a plan the Composer cuts, then issues, then the Workshop builds |

The two are never run in one pass. The meta-auditorium is Diamond 1 across fields; the Deep Auditorium
stays the Diamond 2 tool, and it stays replayable one move at a time.

**Two products, not one.** Diamond 2 across many fields produces a map (the per-field tables, kept whole
and read together as stimulus) and a plan (a short list he cuts, each step carrying its purpose and its
dependencies). A single ranked table across all fields would be about a hundred rows, and a table that long
is scrolled, not judged.

## Two risks, stated before the first use

1. **A model asked Why produces plausible reasons.** This is the failure the i-have-adhd study found
   (`method/measuring-presentation.md`): a rule pushed the model to name a cause the evidence did not
   support. So Diamond 1 rests on recorded evidence, his rulings, SESSION_LOG and past complaints, and every
   answer names its evidence or is marked an assumption. The User Researcher sits in it even where the
   topic table does not convene that chair.
2. **The purpose is his.** The quorum proposes problem statements; the Composer rules. All chairs are one
   model, so their agreement is not corroboration, and a Why diamond they close alone would be one more
   place where a model grades itself.

## The first run — the presentation gate, 2026-11-10

**Diamond 1.** Eight chairs asked one Why each (the seven of the presentation topic plus the User
Researcher), against evidence in his Key and the library. It converged to three problem statements:
1. **Detection.** The only detector of a presentation defect is the Composer, after the fact.
2. **Unit.** The gate measures the form of a message; his stated purpose is what he can decide or do, and
   no check asks how many decisions a message hands him.
3. **Accumulation.** Rules join without a way to retire them or to find where two collide.

He kept statement 2 as the brief. The other two went to the record.

**Diamond 2.** One thinker call diverged against that brief (three ideas for each of seven chairs). The
quorum, which is this session, merged them into nine rows. **The second thinker's audit was not run, so the
Must and Nice labels are one model's reading.** Three findings carry the plan:
- The brief had no definition of *decision*, no baseline and no direction. So the plan starts with two
  measurement steps and not a new rule.
- A hook counting `[DECIDE]` markers was recycled: the model writes both the tag and the message, so it
  would measure obedience to tagging, not decisions handed. Counts come from the Composer's labels.
- Two rows would have overturned his own rulings (the count leading the first line; dropping the board
  line on a zero-decision turn). He kept both rulings.

## The definition that came out of it

Labels borrow existing terms (not invented): the RAID log's **Decision** and **Action** as separate things;
the library's own six endings (`protocols/endings.md`) for what a turn ends on; speech-act theory's
directive and commissive for edge cases (a request or proposal hands the reader something; "I'll run it" or
"it is merged" hands nothing). DACI and RAPID agree on the split: the one who decides is not the one who
performs. Four rulings by the Composer settled what the terms left open:
1. Decide and Do are counted apart. A decision is an authorization, a question or a doubt that asks him to
   rule; a Do is an item of his task list or a Use Case.
2. Anything asked of him counts, whether it comes in a buttons box or in prose.
3. A call he is asked to check counts as one decision each.
4. A choice embedded in a task step counts as a decision.

Two further rulings: an open question with no options is not a decision, and an item mentioned as coming
up but not asked of him does not count.

## The baseline, as measured

26 past assistant messages from four instances' sessions were drawn by a fixed seed from long messages in
the machine's own transcripts (12, 8, 5 and 1 from the four sources; the quota for the fourth was 5, and only one qualified), with credential-shaped text filtered out.
**The session labeled them under his rulings; he ruled on the cases the rules left open and did not label
each message.** So the labels are the session's, and the ground truth is partly one model's. Counts, with the
last two rulings applied:
- 7 of the 26 hand him nothing.
- 16 of the 26 hand at least one decision, 7 of those without a buttons box. Three of the 7 are task lists,
  exempt by design; four ask in prose.
- 15 of the 16 hand exactly one decision. One hands three. One decision per message is what already
  happens, so that candidate rule describes the sample and adds nothing.
- Of the first ten messages, four (#4, 7, 9 and 10) needed a ruling the plain definition did not give. The
  26 together carry 18 decisions and 14 actions.

**Limits.** The first extract missed the buttons questions (it counted 1 of 10 and the true figure was 97 of
204 long messages in the wider pool) and was corrected before anything was labeled. The sample is not
stratified by task type. Sources are practitioner and vendor pages, plus the abstracts of the speech-act
papers; no authoritative standard for a fixed set of meeting-minute categories was found.

## The second run — Strategy, 2026-11-10

**Diamond 1.** Four chairs (Product Owner, User Researcher, Software Architect, Agile Facilitator) asked one
Why each against the Composer's Key, the floor of this repository and SESSION_LOG. Three problem
statements: **Audience** (the estate is built for one verified person and designed for many; no study of a
second user was found), **Handoff cost** (the researcher/builder split costs several hops per study and none
is counted) and **Continuity** (the estate exists so nothing is lost between sessions, yet losses are found
after the fact, by the Composer). He kept Continuity as the brief.

**Diamond 2.** The quorum merged seven rows; a second thinker, working apart on the purpose and the table
only, audited them. Its findings changed the plan:
- The first plan would have missed two of the three known losses, because it scanned for deferral phrases
  and the other two were an artifact made and never filed and a request never built. Extraction must cover
  three kinds: deferrals, things the Composer asked for, things an instance produced.
- A count nothing writes down stays unknown, and a detected item with no card is detected and still lost.
  The count goes into the log and each item is filed in the same closing act.
- A low count means *not seen*, not *not lost*. The Composer's spot-check stays the ground truth.

**One hand-run test.** Run on a session believed clean, the extraction returned no candidates. The session
held 2,024 characters of assistant text, six button answers and one typed request: **the Composer's words
arrive mostly as button answers (tool results), so a scanner that reads only message text misses most of
them.** The transcript that held the three known losses no longer exists on disk, so recall cannot be tested
on it; this session's own unfiled items served instead, which is circular.

**One Why across two fields.** The presentation gate's *Detection* and Strategy's *Continuity* are the same
problem: the Composer is the only detector, after the fact. Two fields found it independently. That is the
signal a cross-field diamond exists to watch for, and it is one occurrence.

**A wording correction.** "A deferral with no card is a drop" appears in this library's own log as a
sentence of the Library. It is not a ruling of the Composer; a plan that relies on it needs him to make it
one.

## What is not decided

- **Ruled afterwards, 2026-11-10, standing:** a ceiling of one decision per message with no floor, a batch
  he asked for exempt (`protocols/presentation-checklist.md`). The direction question is closed; the
  counter that enforces it is not built.
- **Round 2 on the presentation plan**, the second thinker's audit, was not run (it was run on Strategy).
- **The tooling.** A count over the transcripts, a log of decisions per message and a periodic sample for
  him to label are the Workshop's to build; none exists.
