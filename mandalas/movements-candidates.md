# Movement candidates — evidence for eleven borders, and the chairs the twelve cannot reach

**`mandalas/movements-layer.md` argues exactly one movement↔chair border and refuses the other
eleven, for a reason this page does not overturn:** *placing a chair in a lineage is a judgement,
and judgements in this corpus belong to the Composer.* **A session may assemble dated candidate
evidence. It may not assign one.**

**This page is the first half and only the first half.** Nothing below is a placement. Every row is
evidence offered so a ruling can be made against something rather than against a feeling.

## What counts as evidence here

**A date on both sides, or it is not a candidate.**

```
chair side       the chair's own function, as this corpus already defines it
movement side    a dated artifact — a manifesto, a specification, a named role,
                 a published book — not a vibe and not a resemblance
```

**A resemblance is not evidence.** Every chair resembles every movement a little, because both are
ways of working. What earns a row is that the movement produced *the thing the chair does* at a
date that can be named.

Software Architect ↔ contract-first API design   a practice, not a movement

⚠️ **A date on both sides makes a candidate ADMISSIBLE. It does not make the two things the same
kind of object.** That is the second bar, and it caught the two rows whose movement-side evidence
was strongest — SRE is the best-dated entry on this page, named at Google in 2003 and booked in
2016, and it fell anyway. **Strength of evidence and correctness of scale are independent, and this
page had only been measuring the first.**

**What passes it:** a movement is a lineage that crosses the craft and that more than one chair could
plausibly stand in. What fails it: a practice, a role, or a discipline bounded to one chair's own
territory — however well dated.

**The SRE rejection was taken in a second pass**, after the row was first recorded as unruled rather
than inferred from its absence. The verdict is his; the delay is the method working.

## The two that are contested, and the contest is the finding

**Agile Auditor ↔ Lean or Agile.** Lean's [genchi genbutsu](https://www.lean.org/lexicon-terms/genchi-genbutsu/) — *go and see for
yourself* — is the auditor's method exactly, and waste is the auditor's subject. **But the chair
audits agility**, which points at Agile. **Both have a date and they pull in different directions**,
and the graph already records `agile succeeds lean`, so the two are not alternatives so much as a
question of *where on the lineage the chair stands*.

**QA Engineer ↔ Lean or Agile.** Lean's *jidoka* builds quality in rather than inspecting it after —
which is the modern QA position. Agile testing is the nearer literature. **Same shape as above, same
unresolved direction.**

⚠️ **Neither is weak evidence. Both are two pieces of good evidence disagreeing**, which is a
different thing and should not be resolved by picking the tidier one.

**Ruled by the Composer, 2026-09-08: they SPLIT. Agile Auditor → Lean, QA Engineer → Agile.**

**And the ruling refuses the premise both rows shared.** The page presented them as *the same shape,
the same unresolved direction* — one question asked twice. They are not. **The Auditor's method is
go-and-see, which is Lean's** whatever it is auditing; **QA's practices in this estate are Agile's**,
whatever their Lean ancestry. The chairs sit at different points on a lineage the graph already
records as `agile succeeds lean`, and treating them as a pair was the error that made the contest
look unresolvable.
